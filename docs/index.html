High signal
Exagamglogene autotemcel (Casgevy) is a one-time gene therapy treatment for severe sickle cell disease that modifies a patient's own blood stem cells to produce healthy hemoglobin and reduce painful vaso-occlusive crises. The Breakthrough Therapy designation indicates FDA recognition that this therapy addresses a serious unmet need in patients who currently face lifelong complications, frequent hospitalizations, and shortened life expectancy from severe sickle cell disease. Unlike traditional treatments that manage symptoms through chronic blood transfusions, hydroxyurea, or pain medications, this gene therapy offers a potential functional cure by addressing the underlying genetic defect with a single treatment rather than requiring lifelong daily or monthly interventions.

Lecanemab (Leqembi) (Eisai)
HSAI
FDA Breakthrough Therapy · granted 2019-06-17 · Early Alzheimer's disease
High signal
Lecanemab is a treatment for early Alzheimer's disease that received Breakthrough Therapy designation from the FDA, meaning it showed substantial improvement over existing therapies in treating a serious condition during early clinical testing. This designation matters clinically because it suggests the drug demonstrated meaningful slowing of cognitive decline in patients during the earliest symptomatic stages of Alzheimer's, when intervention may preserve more brain function. Lecanemab is one of the first disease-modifying therapies to demonstrate measurable slowing of Alzheimer's progression in clinical trials, representing a significant advance over prior treatments that only managed symptoms without affecting underlying disease course.

Tabelecleucel (Ebvallo) (Atara Biotherapeutics)
ATRA
EMA PRIME · granted 2016-10-01 · Epstein-Barr virus-positive post-transplant lymphoproliferative disease (EBV+ PTLD)
High signal
Tabelecleucel is a cell therapy designed to treat a dangerous cancer called post-transplant lymphoproliferative disease, which occurs when Epstein-Barr virus causes uncontrolled growth of immune cells in organ transplant recipients. The PRIME designation from European regulators indicates this addresses a critical unmet medical need where current treatment options are limited and often ineffective. This represents a targeted immunotherapy approach for EBV+ PTLD, a condition where standard chemotherapy has poor outcomes and often cannot be used due to transplant patients' already weakened immune systems, making this potentially the first therapy specifically designed for this patient population.
Verify: ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT03394365)Current price
